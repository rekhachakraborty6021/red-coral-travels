Overview
Where clouds come to rest

Established in 1972, Meghalaya ("Abode of Clouds") is one of India's most biodiverse states. Mawsynram and Cherrapunji hold Guinness World Records as the wettest places on Earth — receiving roughly 37 feet of rainfall annually. Yet despite all that rain, the state faces winter water scarcity, as the water drains rapidly into neighboring Bangladesh below.

Culture & Society
A matriarchal world unlike any other

About half of Meghalaya's population belongs to the Khasi tribe — one of the largest matrilineal societies in the world. Women are considered the pillars of society, and this is reflected in every aspect of daily life.

How the Khasi matrilineal system works

Men move to the woman's house after marriage. Surnames are taken from the mother. The youngest daughter inherits the family property. There is no dowry system. Women run most local shops — from vegetables and medicines to meat and liquor.

Sacred Forests & Nature Worship

Meghalaya's deep respect for nature is embodied in its strictly preserved Sacred Forests. Taking even a single dead leaf or stone from these forests is believed to invite a curse. Ancient monoliths inside the forests represent ancestors — standing stones for males, flat stones for females.

Cuisine
Food & local flavours

Meghalaya's food is simple, honest, and deeply tied to its land — built around rice, pork, and fresh forest ingredients. The most universal local habit is chewing Kwai.

Rice & Pork

The state's staple diet. Often prepared with minimal spices, letting the organic quality of ingredients shine.

Kwai (Betel Nut)

A universal post-meal ritual — betel nut eaten with betel leaf and lime, with no added masala.

Bamboo-Based Dishes

Bamboo shoots are cooked into savory preparations, reflecting the state's deep connection to bamboo.

Local Market Food

Fresh vegetables, meats, and local brews sold by women traders in every town and village market.

October–April gives you crystal-clear rivers and open roads. Monsoon months bring muddy waters, fog, and extreme rainfall. September is transitional — rivers start clearing but fog persists.

Practical Tips
Know before you go
🌿

Respect Sacred Forests

Do not take anything out of the Sacred Forests — not a leaf, not a stone. Locals believe a curse will follow.

🥾

Prepare for tough treks

Root bridges and waterfalls require steep, strenuous descents. Good footwear and fitness are essential.

🌧️

Fog & visibility

Self-drive roads can be extremely foggy with very low visibility. Drive slowly and stay off road at night.

🎋

Embrace bamboo culture

Pick up a Kno (basket) or Knup (rain umbrella) — local bamboo crafts that are both beautiful and functional.

Sample Itinerary
5 days in Meghalaya — a first-timer's journey

From the misty highlands of Cherrapunji to the hidden canyons of the Garo Hills — a curated route that covers the full magic of the Abode of Clouds.

Day 1
Day 1 Shillong → Cherrapunji & Root Bridge
Morning
Depart Shillong into the misty Khasi Hills. Begin the descent to Nongriat village via 3,200+ steep steps to reach the legendary Double Decker Living Root Bridge. Strenuous — carry water & good shoes
Afternoon
Climb back up and visit the roaring Seven Sister Waterfall and the layered Wei Sawdong Waterfall through lush forest trails.
Evening
Stop at the mystical Mawphlang Sacred Forest for a guided tour of monoliths and Khasi ritual sites. Leave nothing behind — not even a leaf Return to Shillong for a relaxed cafe dinner.
Day 2
Day 2 Dawki & Jaintia Hills
Morning
Drive to Dawki / Shnongpdeng. Take a boat ride on the Umngot River — so clear the boats appear to float on glass. Visit the nearby single-decker living root bridge. Best Oct–April for clear water
Afternoon
Head into the Jaintia Hills to swim and boat at Krang Suri Waterfall (entry ₹50). Cross the river by boat (₹100) to hike to the gorgeous Phe Phe (Fifi) Waterfalls.
Evening
Drive to Kongthong — The Whistling Village. Spend the evening with locals who call each other using unique tunes sung by their mothers at birth.
Day 3
Day 3 Caves & Journey to Garo Hills
Morning
Explore the deep, natural river cave of Krem Chympe in the Jaintia Hills — one of India's most impressive underground cave systems.
Afternoon
Begin the long scenic drive westward into the Garo Hills. Watch traditional bamboo houses and villages roll by along the misty road.
Evening
Arrive at Siju Village. Visit the famous Siju Cave briefly, then head to Emangre Village to check into a local bamboo homestay for the night.
Day 4
Day 4 Wari Chora Canyon
Morning
Wake early. Hire a local 4x4 vehicle (~₹3,000) to tackle the 15 km bumped road to the Wari Chora parking spot. 4x4 mandatory — normal cars cannot pass
Afternoon
Trek through dense forest to the river. Board an inflatable raft to float through the towering, hidden Wari Chora Canyon — breathtaking walls, secret waterfalls, and complete wilderness.
Evening
Trek back, return to Emangre. Wind down at the peaceful local Fish Sanctuary nearby before a quiet bamboo homestay dinner.
Day 5
Day 5 Culture, Cuisine & Departure
Morning
Take a village tour and observe the matriarchal Khasi society in action — visit local markets run entirely by women selling vegetables, meats, and traditional goods.
Afternoon
Enjoy a traditional pork and rice meal. Try Kwai (betel nut with leaf and lime) as a post-meal cultural experience shared by almost every local.
Evening
Pick up handmade bamboo souvenirs — a Kno (woven basket) or a Knup (hands-free bamboo rain umbrella). Conclude your Meghalaya journey.

Trip summary

This 5-day route takes you from the misty waterfalls and living root bridges of Cherrapunji → the glass-bottom rivers and whistling villages of Dawki & Kongthong → the underground caves of the Jaintia Hills → the raw canyon wilderness of the Garo Hills. Best done between October and April for clear rivers and safe roads. Ideal for adventurers and curious travellers seeking something truly off the beaten path.
