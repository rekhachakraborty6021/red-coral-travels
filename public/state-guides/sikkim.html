Overview
India's Second Smallest — and Most Serene

Sikkim borders Tibet, Bhutan, Nepal, and West Bengal — a tiny Himalayan state widely described as "heaven" for its crystal rivers, snow-capped peaks, and deep Buddhist culture. It is one of India's most environmentally progressive states, with a total ban on chemical farming and a deep collective commitment to organic living.

🌿

India's only 100% organic state

Sikkim's farmers use zero chemical fertilizers or pesticides — every fruit, vegetable, and grain is grown organically. Meals here aren't just fresh; they're genuinely different in taste and quality from anything found in the rest of India.

Geography & Regions
Four Districts — Four Different Worlds

Sikkim is split into four districts, each with a completely different character — from the buzzing capital of Gangtok to the extreme high-altitude wilderness of North Sikkim.

East Sikkim

Gangtok & Nathu La

The capital, M.G. Marg, Tsomgo Lake, the Indo-China border, and the Baba Temple.

North Sikkim — largest

Lachen & Lachung

Extreme altitudes, Gurudongmar Lake (17,800ft), Zero Point, Yumthang Valley of Flowers.

West Sikkim

Pelling & Monasteries

Closest Kanchenjunga views, Pelling Skywalk, Pemayangtse Monastery, Rabdentse Ruins.

South Sikkim

Ravangla & Namchi

Buddha Park, Namchi Chardham replicas, tea estates, and panoramic mountain views.

📋

Permits required for North Sikkim & Nathu La

Traveling to Nathu La Pass, Lachen, or Lachung requires Inner Line Permits — arranged through your local taxi driver or agent in Gangtok. Self-driving is strongly discouraged on these restricted, high-altitude routes. Children under 5 are not permitted at Nathu La. Photography is strictly banned at the Indo-China border.

Culture & Traditions
Buddhism, Butter Lamps & Organic Living

Sikkim's culture is defined by its deep Buddhist identity — monasteries, spinning prayer wheels, Thanka paintings, and butter lamps are present in every corner of the state. The people live in quiet harmony with the land, reflected in both their farming practices and their cuisine.

🛕

Buddhist immersion

Spin the prayer wheels at Do-drul Chorten, meditate at Ravangla Buddha Park, or sit in silence at the 1614-built Pemayangtse Monastery.

🏔️

Sacred mountain reverence

Kanchenjunga is worshipped as a guardian deity. It's visible from Tashi View Point, Pelling, and South Sikkim at sunrise.

🏡

Bhutia homestay life

Staying with a local Bhutia or Nepali family — sitting by the chulha (hearth), eating homemade organic food — is the heart of the Sikkim experience.

Attractions
What to See & Experience
17,800 ft — Holy Lake

Gurudongmar Lake

A sacred high-altitude lake that miraculously never fully freezes, even in peak winter. Depart Lachen by 4 AM. Leave before 11 AM as extreme winds arrive.

15,300 ft — Snow

Zero Point (Yumesamdong)

The last accessible civilian point near the Tibet border — a snow-covered plateau where the road simply ends and the mountains begin.

Valley of Flowers

Yumthang Valley

The Teesta River flowing through a mountain valley that blooms with wildflowers in summer — one of North Sikkim's most beautiful landscapes.

En route — North Sikkim

Seven Sisters & Naga Waterfalls

Spectacular waterfalls visible from the road on the drive into North Sikkim — dramatic cascades through dense mountain forest.

Bhutia Homestay

Lachen & Lachung Villages

Base camp towns at 8,800–9,000 ft — stay in traditional Bhutia homestays, eat organic meals, and experience authentic high-altitude village life.

North Sikkim Road

Bheem Nala Waterfall

Also called the "Amitabh Bachchan Falls" for a Bollywood scene filmed here — a towering waterfall visible on the drive between Lachen and Lachung.

14,400 ft — China Border

Nathu La Pass

The heavily guarded Indo-China border — piercing cold winds, Indian Army presence, and the extraordinary reality of standing at an international frontier. No photography permitted.

High Altitude Lake

Tsomgo (Changu) Lake

A crystal-clear lake that freezes solid in winter — serene in summer, magical under snow. Located en route to Nathu La Pass.

Army Shrine

Baba Harbhajan Singh Temple

A revered shrine to an Indian Army soldier whose spirit is believed to still protect the border. Soldiers and tourists alike pay their respects here.

Gangtok

Tashi View Point

The best place in Gangtok for a clear morning view of Mount Kanchenjunga — arrive before sunrise for golden light on the peak.

Gangtok — City Life

M.G. Marg

Gangtok's pedestrian-only central street — cafes, shops, street food, and the pulse of daily city life in Sikkim's capital.

Gangtok

Namgyal Institute of Tibetology

Established 1958 — houses the world's third-largest collection of Buddhist texts. Essential for understanding Sikkim's Tibetan cultural heritage.

West Sikkim — Pelling

Pelling Skywalk & Chenrezig

A thrilling glass walkway leading to a 130-ft Chenrezig statue — with the Kanchenjunga range as a backdrop, this is one of Sikkim's most dramatic viewpoints.

West Sikkim — 1614 AD

Pemayangtse Monastery

One of Sikkim's oldest monasteries at 400+ years. Quiet, remote, and architecturally extraordinary.

West Sikkim — Ruins

Rabdentse Ruins

The ruins of Sikkim's second capital, destroyed in a Nepali invasion in 1840. Now a scenic garden with panoramic views.

South Sikkim — Ravangla

Buddha Park

A massive 130-ft Buddha statue set in a peaceful meditation park — the spiritual centrepiece of South Sikkim.

South Sikkim — Namchi

Namchi Chardham

Replicas of India's four Char Dham pilgrimage sites and all 12 Jyotirlingas — a complete Hindu spiritual complex in the mountains.

South Sikkim

Tea Estates

Roing-style tea gardens across South Sikkim — the organic equivalent of Assam's famous estates, set against mountain backdrops.

Cuisine
Organic, Unique & Deeply Himalayan

Sikkimese food is built from ingredients unavailable anywhere else in India — fermented mountain greens, yak milk cheese, buckwheat rolls, and noodle soups eaten at altitude after long days in the snow.

Unique local ingredients

Chhurpi

Hardened yak milk cheese — chewed slowly, earthy and protein-rich.

Gundruk

Fermented mustard greens — tangy, sour, and deeply local.

Kinema

Fermented soybeans — nutty and strong, used in curries.

Iskus ko Munta

Tender squash leaves — stir-fried simply with local spices.

Must-try dishes

Nepali Thali

The traditional Sikkimese meal — rice, lentils, greens, pickle, and seasonal vegetables from the farm.

Thukpa

Hearty Tibetan noodle soup — perfect after a cold day at altitude.

Sel Roti & Aloo Dum

A deep-fried sweet rice-flour ring served with spiced potato curry — a beloved street snack.

Typhoo (Giant Momo)

An oversized stuffed dumpling — a Sikkim original, larger and more filled than a standard momo.

Khuri

Buckwheat rolls stuffed with chhurpi and potatoes — a Bhutia homestay speciality.

Butter Tea

Churned mountain herbs, milk, and ghee — warming, unusual, and completely essential at high altitude.

When to Visit
Best time of year

March – May

Peak season. Flowers bloom, skies clear, passes open. Best all-round window.

Oct – Dec

Second peak. Clear mountain views, crisp air, festive Sikkimese culture.

Dec – Feb (Snow)

Heavy snow at altitude. Gurudongmar roads may close. Best for snow lovers.

June – Aug (Avoid)

Severe monsoon landslides, muddy roads, and low visibility. High risk.

Sun sets at 4:30 PM — plan accordingly

Start every day by 7–8 AM to make full use of daylight. High-altitude drives take longer than expected.

Practical Tips
Know Before You Go
🫁

Altitude sickness is real

At Nathu La and Gurudongmar, breathlessness is common. Carry camphor to smell, drink water continuously, and spend no more than 30–60 minutes at extreme peaks.

⏰

4:30 PM sunset — start at 7 AM

Days are short at altitude. Start driving by 7–8 AM every day. The Gurudongmar trip departs Lachen at 4 AM and must leave the lake by 11 AM.

👶

Children under 5 cannot visit Nathu La

Extreme altitude conditions at the Indo-China border make it unsafe for very young children. Plan your family itinerary around this restriction.

📵

No photos at Nathu La

Mobile recording and photography are strictly banned at the Indo-China border at Nathu La Pass. No exceptions — it is an active military zone.

Sample Itinerary — Gangtok & North Sikkim Circuit
5 Days in Sikkim

From Gangtok's vibrant streets to the holy waters of Gurudongmar at 17,800 feet — the classic first-timer's circuit through East and North Sikkim.

Day 1
Day 1 Gangtok — Arrival & City Culture
Morning
Arrive at Bagdogra airport or NJP station. Take the scenic 4-hour taxi ride up into the hills to Gangtok. Check in near M.G. Marg. ~4 hrs from Bagdogra / NJP
Afternoon
Visit Banjhakri Waterfalls theme park, then take the Gangtok Ropeway for a bird's-eye view of the capital city spread across the hillside.
Evening
Walk the pedestrian-only M.G. Marg — cafes, shops, and the pulse of Gangtok. Try a traditional Nepali Thali or street snacks like Sel Roti and Aloo Dum. Arrange Nathu La permits tonight
Day 2
Day 2 East Sikkim — Border, Temple & Lake
Early AM
Head to Tashi View Point before sunrise for a clear, golden view of Mount Kanchenjunga. Then begin the steep permitted drive to Nathu La Pass at 14,400 ft — bundle up for piercing winds. No photography at the border — strictly enforced
Afternoon
Descend to pay respects at the Baba Harbhajan Singh Temple, then spend time marvelling at the crystal-clear (or frozen) Tsomgo (Changu) Lake.
Evening
Return to Gangtok. Warm up at a local cafe with Chicken Thenthuk or Shyaphale. Rest — tomorrow is a long drive north. Under-5s cannot visit Nathu La
Day 3
Day 3 Journey into North Sikkim — Lachen
Morning
Check out early and begin the scenic 6–7 hour drive towards North Sikkim. Stop to photograph the spectacular Seven Sisters Waterfall and Naga Waterfall from the road. ~125 km, 6–7 hrs — start by 7 AM
Afternoon
Stop in Mangan for a simple organic lunch at a roadside dhaba. Cross the Chungthang dam and continue into the high-altitude town of Lachen at 8,800 ft.
Evening
Check into a Bhutia homestay. Sit by the chulha and share a deeply traditional dinner of Khuri, Gundruk soup, and Butter Tea. Sleep early — tomorrow departs at 4 AM. 4 AM departure tomorrow — no exceptions
Day 4
Day 4 Gurudongmar Lake & Lachung
4 AM
Depart Lachen in darkness. Drive through the Thangu Valley checkpost to reach Gurudongmar Lake at 17,800 ft — walk gently, smell camphor if breathless, and stand before the holy partially-unfrozen waters. Leave before 11 AM — extreme winds arrive
Afternoon
Return to Lachen for a quick lunch. Drive toward Lachung (9,000 ft), stopping to view the towering Bheem Nala (Amitabh Bachchan) Waterfall.
Evening
Arrive in Lachung. Check into your hotel, enjoy a mild-spiced organic Sikkimese dinner, and rest fully. The altitude and long drive will be exhausting. Drink water — altitude dehydration is common
Day 5
Day 5 Zero Point, Valley of Flowers & Departure
Early AM
Drive through the Yumthang Valley (Valley of Flowers) with the Teesta River winding through the mountains. Continue up to Zero Point at 15,300 ft — the last point on the civilian road, blanketed in snow.
Afternoon
Descend back through Lachung for a quick meal, then begin the long 8-hour scenic drive back to Gangtok. ~8 hrs — start the descent by noon
Evening
Arrive in Gangtok. Collect belongings and proceed to NJP or Bagdogra for your journey home — concluding one of India's most extraordinary Himalayan adventures.

Trip summary

This 5-day Gangtok–North Sikkim circuit takes you from the vibrant streets of Gangtok → the Indo-China border at Nathu La (14,400 ft) → the traditional Bhutia homestays of Lachen (8,800 ft) → the holy waters of Gurudongmar Lake (17,800 ft) → the snow plains of Zero Point (15,300 ft). Always arrange permits in advance, carry camphor for altitude, and start every day by 7 AM. Best visited March–May or October–December.
