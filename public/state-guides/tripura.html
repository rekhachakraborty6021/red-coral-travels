Overview
Where royalty meets the Bangladesh border

Tripura is one of Northeast India's most accessible and historically rich states. Its capital Agartala — the largest city in the state — sits beautifully on the banks of the Howrah River, just 2 kilometres from the Bangladesh border. It is a city shaped by royal vision, with its roads, markets, and public buildings all planned by the Manikya maharajas.

Capital: Agartala 2 km from Bangladesh ~94% Hindu population 3 official languages Direct flights from Delhi & Kolkata
🌏

India's closest city to Bangladesh

Agartala is just 2 kilometres from the Bangladesh border — making it geographically unique among all Indian state capitals. Visitors should be mindful of border area regulations when exploring the city's outskirts.

History & Heritage
The Manikya Dynasty — builders of Agartala

Agartala's entire identity is shaped by the Manikya royal lineage — a dynasty that chose, planned, and built the city we see today. Every palace, road, and public building carries their legacy.

Pre-19th C

Capital at Rangamati (Udaipur)

The original capital of the Tripura kingdom, located in the Udaipur region of Northern Tripura.

19th C

Maharaja Krishna Kishore shifts the capital

To escape continuous attacks by the Kuki tribe, the capital was relocated from Rangamati to Agartala — a decision that shaped the entire state.

1901

Ujjayanta Palace is built

The crown jewel of Agartala — a palace blending Mughal domes with European colonnades, built as the royal seat of the Manikya dynasty.

1940

Maharaja Bir Bikram Kishore redesigns the city

The modern layout of Agartala — its roads, markets, and municipal buildings — was meticulously planned by Maharaja Bir Bikram Kishore Manikya Bahadur.

Built 1901

Ujjayanta Palace

The architectural crown of Tripura — a sprawling royal palace that blends two distinct traditions in one extraordinary structure. Built by the Manikya dynasty as their royal seat, it now stands as Agartala's most visited monument.

Agartala

Rajbari Palace

Narangirudra Temple

A notable religious site in Agartala — a calm and significant stop in the city's heritage trail.

Agartala — River

The river on which Agartala was built — perfect for a peaceful morning walk, especially with a view of the city's skyline and the Bangladesh plains beyond.

Year-Round

Vibrant fairs held throughout the year showcasing Tripura's rich artisanal traditions — weaving, bamboo craft, and tribal textile work.

Cuisine
Fish, street food & a vibrant night culture

Tripura's food identity is built around fish — a reflection of the rivers and waterways that define the region. At night, Agartala transforms into a lively street food destination with a fascinating blend of Indian and Chinese flavours.

Mui Borok — the soul of Tripuri cuisine

A traditional fish preparation eaten with great enthusiasm across the state. Made with locally sourced fish and prepared with regional herbs and spices, Mui Borok is the single most important dish to try in Tripura.

Traditional Fish Dishes

Fish is the cornerstone of Tripuri cuisine — fresh, local varieties cooked in simple, flavourful preparations.

Indian Street Food

The night streets of Agartala light up with vendors serving a range of Indian snacks and street staples.

Chinese Street Food

A surprising and beloved feature of Agartala's night market — Chinese-influenced stalls blend naturally into the street food scene.

🌙

Agartala's night streets come alive

After dark, the streets of Agartala transform — food stalls light up, vendors set out, and the city's most vibrant social life begins. Walking the night market is one of the best things to do in Tripura.

Practical Tips
Know before you go
🌏

Respect border proximity

Agartala is 2 km from Bangladesh. Be aware of border area regulations — particularly in the outskirts and near the Howrah River.

🐟

Try Mui Borok — always

The traditional Tripuri fish dish is non-negotiable. Ask your hotel or any local restaurant for their preparation.

🌙

Walk the night streets

Agartala's best food and social culture happens after dark. Make sure to explore the lit-up streets on at least one evening.

Sample Itinerary
3 days in Tripura — a first-timer's journey

From the 1901 palace of the Manikya dynasty to the glowing night streets of Agartala — three days in the royal heart of Northeast India.

Day 1
Day 1 Agartala — Heritage & Palaces
Morning
Arrive at Agartala Airport or Railway Station — one of the most accessible capitals in the Northeast, with direct flights from Delhi and Kolkata. Check into your accommodation near the city centre. No ILP or special permits required
Afternoon
Begin at the grand Ujjayanta Palace (1901) — admire the extraordinary Mughal-meets-European architecture and learn about the Manikya dynasty's reign. Continue to Rajbari Palace and seek blessings at the Narangirudra Temple. Built 1901 — Mughal-European fusion
Evening
Head into the night streets of Agartala — stalls lit up, vendors calling out, Indian and Chinese street food filling the air. Try the traditional Mui Borok fish dish — the single most essential culinary experience in Tripura. Night market is the highlight
Day 2
Day 2 Culture, River & Handicrafts
Morning
Take a peaceful morning walk along the Howrah River banks — the very river on which Agartala was built. Stand and contemplate that Bangladesh is just 2 km away from where you're standing. Border area regulations apply nearby
Afternoon
Dedicate the afternoon to Agartala's vibrant arts scene — visit local markets or attend one of the many handicraft fairs hosted year-round, showcasing tribal weaving, bamboo crafts, and traditional Tripuri textile work. Fairs run year-round — check dates on arrival
Evening
Rent a bike and cycle through the city — observe the meticulously planned roads and municipal buildings laid out by Maharaja Bir Bikram Kishore Manikya Bahadur in 1940. Every street tells a royal story.
Day 3
Day 3 Nature, Culture & Departure
Morning
Venture slightly out of the city for some outdoor adventure — hiking, biking, or wildlife spotting in the natural areas surrounding Agartala. The terrain is gentle and accessible without special equipment.
Afternoon
Return to the city for a final lunch. Soak in the multilingual atmosphere — Bengali, Kokborok, English, Hindi, and Chakma spoken around you simultaneously — a living reminder of Tripura's remarkable cultural layering.
Evening
Pack your bags and depart from Agartala Airport or Railway Station on a direct flight or train back to your home city. Direct flights to Delhi, Kolkata & Chennai

Trip summary

Tripura is Northeast India's most accessible and underrated royal destination. Three days in Agartala takes you through the Ujjayanta Palace (1901), the banks of the Howrah River, year-round handicraft fairs, and the glowing night food streets — all in a city that sits just 2 km from the Bangladesh border. No ILP required. Direct flights available. The most logistically effortless entry point into Northeast India.
