Overview
An unexplored paradise of valleys & peaks

Manipur sits at the eastern edge of India, sharing a border with Myanmar. Though it attracts fewer tourists than other states, it is a true paradise for those who seek nature, culture, and solitude. Its capital, Imphal, rests in a lush valley at 786 meters above sea level — giving the entire state a pleasant, mountain-like climate year-round.

Culture & Society
Diverse, proud, and untouched

Manipur's culture is remarkably distinct from the rest of India. The primary community in the capital is the Meitei people. Rural villages preserve a way of life completely untouched by outside influence — people rely on agriculture, forestry, and crafting items from wood and bamboo.

Hinduism ~70%
Meitei (indigenous) ~14%
Christianity ~10.5%
Kuki & others ~5.5%

Village Life — the state's greatest attraction

Manipur's rural villages are considered one of its biggest draws. Away from urban influence, the locals live in deep connection with the forest — foraging herbs for food and medicine, crafting items from bamboo, and preserving traditions passed down for generations.

Ima Market — Asia's largest all-women market

Located in Imphal, the Ima Market ("Mother's Market") is run by 5,000 to 6,000 women vendors and is the only market of its kind in Asia. It sells everything from fresh vegetables and fish to handlooms, handicrafts, and traditional Manipuri textiles.

Handloom & Handicrafts

Manipur is renowned for its fine handwoven textiles and bamboo crafts. The Ima Market and local artisans are the best places to shop.

Cuisine
Food & local flavours

Manipuri food is deeply unique — flavored by ingredients foraged directly from the forests, many of which also serve as traditional medicines. It is unlike anything found elsewhere in India.

Forest Herb Cuisine

Local dishes incorporate herbs and greens sourced directly from surrounding forests, blending food and traditional medicine.

Rice-Based Staples

Rice is the foundation of every Manipuri meal, paired with organic vegetables, fish, and forest ingredients.

Bamboo & Wood Crafts

Not food — but bamboo items like baskets and utensils are woven into daily life and make for beautiful souvenirs.

Ima Market Produce

The freshest local vegetables, fish, and meats sold daily by women vendors — the heart of Imphal's food culture.

Because Manipur sits at a higher altitude, it enjoys pleasant weather all year round. There is no bad season to visit — every month offers comfortable temperatures and beautiful green landscapes.

Getting There & Around

🚂

🚕

Local Taxis

Easily available from Imphal to all major sites. Look for MN-01 plates.

Practical Tips
Know before you go
✈️

Fly into Imphal

Avoid the 8–10 hour road journey from other Northeast cities — fly directly into Tulihal Airport and save a full day.

🚕

Use local taxis

Local taxis are readily available from Imphal to all major sites including Keibul Lamjao and Loktak Lake.

🏡

Book floating homestay early

The floating homestays on Loktak Lake are a unique experience — book in advance as they are limited and popular.

🌿

Respect village culture

Rural Manipuri villages are untouched by tourism — approach with humility and ask before photographing locals.

Sample Itinerary
4 days in Manipur — a first-timer's journey

From the ancient walls of Kangla Fort to the floating grasslands of Loktak Lake — a complete introduction to Manipur's culture, wildlife, and landscapes.

Day 1
Day 1 Imphal — Heritage & Culture
Morning
Begin at the historic Kangla Fort & Palace, the ancient seat of the Manipur Kingdom dating to 1632, set along the Imphal River. Continue to the State Museum for a deep dive into Manipuri history. Fly in — skip the 8–10 hr road trip
Afternoon
Visit the Shri Govindaji Temple and explore the religious sites in the Pakhangba area. Enjoy a traditional Manipuri lunch made with forest herbs and organic ingredients.
Evening
Explore the bustling Ima Market — Asia's largest women-only market with over 5,000 female vendors. Browse handlooms, handicrafts, and traditional Manipuri textiles. Best in the late afternoon when fully active
Day 2
Day 2 Bishnupur — Wildlife & Loktak Lake
Morning
Take a taxi ~47 km south to Keibul Lamjao National Park in the Bishnupur district — the world's only floating national park — to spot the rare Sangai deer and diverse birdlife. ~1 hour from Imphal
Afternoon
Explore the adjacent Loktak Lake and its unique circular floating grass and plant islands (phumdis). Visit the nearby Bishnupur Temple.
Evening
Check into a floating homestay on Loktak Lake for a truly one-of-a-kind overnight experience — floating on water, surrounded by phumdis and birdcalls. Book well in advance
Day 3
Day 3 Senapati — Forests & Tribal Villages
Morning
Depart early for the Senapati District in the Naga Hills — a ~3.5 to 4 hour drive (~160 km) north of Imphal through scenic mountain roads. Depart by 7 AM for a full day
Afternoon
Spend the afternoon exploring dense forests, waterfalls, and mountain rivers in one of Manipur's most beautiful districts, surrounded by the songs of local birds.
Evening
Visit rural tribal villages in the Senapati area — experience a way of life completely untouched by outside influence. Stay overnight locally or return to Imphal.
Day 4
Day 4 King Peak & Departure
Morning
Head towards King Peak — Manipur's highest mountain at 3,114 meters above sea level. The journey through green hills and flower-filled meadows is itself a highlight.
Afternoon
Enjoy a relaxing hike or picnic at the summit, taking in 360° panoramic views of Manipur's endless green valleys. A perfect ending to the journey.
Evening
Travel back to Imphal city to rest before your departure from Tulihal International Airport. Airport is only 8 km from the city centre

Trip summary

This 4-day route covers the full range of Manipur — from the ancient heritage of Imphal's Kangla Fort and Ima Market → the floating wilderness of Loktak Lake and Keibul Lamjao → the pristine tribal forests of Senapati → the summit views of King Peak. Because of its high altitude, Manipur is pleasant year-round — making it one of the few Northeast states with no bad season to visit.
